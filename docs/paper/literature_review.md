## TL;DR
- The two argument lines are well supported by verifiable, high-quality literature: (A) action gaming produces broad but genre-specific cognitive benefits with a plausible control-network substrate, and (B) resting-state functional connectivity is a defensible read-out of experience-driven, trait-like network reconfiguration even at small N, provided the claim is framed as within-sample group classification rather than a brain-wide association study.
- Several draft references needed correction or verification: the Brain Sciences 2024 paper is Cahill, Jordan and Dhamala and is task-based/diffusion (not resting-state); "Reineberg 2018 Nature Communications" does not exist and must be replaced; and a few items (Brilhault 2020, West 2015, Focker 2018/2019, Benady-Chorney 2020, Menon and D'Esposito 2022, Gleich 2017, Watanabe 2015) could not be fully verified within budget and are flagged for confirmation before submission.
- The strongest single move for preempting reviewers is to cite Marek 2022 and then explicitly distinguish underpowered brain-wide association correlations from the present group-contrast design, while leaning on Gratton 2018, Cole 2014, Tavor 2016 and Finn 2015 to justify why intrinsic connectivity is trait-like and experience-shaped.

## Key Findings
- Broad transfer from action gaming is real but modest and contested. The best pro evidence is Bediou et al. 2018 (cross-sectional g = 0.55; intervention g = 0.34) and Bediou et al. 2023 (cross-sectional g = 0.64, 95% CI [0.53, 0.74]; intervention g = 0.30, 95% CI [0.11, 0.50]), strongest for top-down attention. The strongest skeptic case is Sala, Tatlidil and Gobet 2018. Both the pro and con headline papers appeared in Psychological Bulletin, so the debate must be presented even-handedly.
- Genre specificity is a load-bearing premise and is defensible, but the field is actively moving from genre labels toward game-mechanics classification (Dale and Green 2017; Bavelier and Green 2019; Dale et al. 2020). Frame the action gamers by their mechanics and load profile, not by the marketing label.
- Resting-state gamer studies converge on exactly the networks the manuscript emphasizes: Gong et al. 2015 report increased intrinsic connectivity between attentional and sensorimotor networks centered on the left insula, and Cahill et al. 2024 link dorsal-stream connectivity to response time.
- The rest-task correspondence and trait-stability literature (Cole 2014, Tavor 2016, Gratton 2018, Finn 2015) is unusually strong and directly licenses using rest to infer experience-shaped, task-relevant architecture.

### TASK A.1 Behavioural evidence for BROAD transfer (and the skeptic replies)

Verified citations:

1. Bediou, B., Adams, D. M., Mayer, R. E., Tipton, E., Green, C. S., and Bavelier, D. (2018). Meta-analysis of action video game impact on perceptual, attentional, and cognitive skills. Psychological Bulletin, 144(1), 77-110. doi:10.1037/bul0000130. Correction: Bediou, B., et al. (2018). Psychological Bulletin, 144(9), 978-979. doi:10.1037/bul0000168.
What it shows: A cross-sectional habitual-player advantage of about half a standard deviation and a smaller causal intervention effect, strongest for top-down attention and spatial cognition. Verbatim: "a positive average effect of about half a standard deviation (g = 0.55)" for cross-sectional studies and "a smaller average effect of a third of a standard deviation (g = 0.34)" for interventions. The authors flag publication bias verbatim: "Publication bias remains, however, a threat with average effects in the published literature estimated to be 30% larger than in the full literature."

2. Bediou, B., Rodgers, M. A., Tipton, E., Mayer, R. E., Green, C. S., and Bavelier, D. (2023). Effects of action video game play on cognitive skills: A meta-analysis. Technology, Mind, and Behavior, 4(1). doi:10.1037/tmb0000102.
What it shows: The updated and larger corpus (105 cross-sectional studies, 221 effect sizes; 28 intervention studies, 91 effect sizes). Verbatim: "action video game players outperformed nonvideo game players in the cross-sectional meta-analysis (large effect, g = 0.64, 95% CI [0.53, 0.74])" and were "causally related to improvements in cognitive skills in the intervention meta-analysis (small effect, g = 0.30, 95% CI [0.11, 0.50])." The paper also reports that "Publication bias was detected in the cross-sectional data set" but "was not detected in the intervention data set," and among intervention domains only top-down attention (g = 0.52) had a confidence interval excluding zero (multitasking g = 0.41).

3. Sala, G., Tatlidil, K. S., and Gobet, F. (2018). Video game training does not enhance cognitive ability: A comprehensive meta-analytic investigation. Psychological Bulletin, 144(2), 111-139. doi:10.1037/bul0000139.
What it shows: Across three meta-analytic models, small or null overall effects and no evidence of a causal video-game-to-cognition relationship once active controls and study quality are accounted for; effect sizes shrink as design quality rises.

4. Sala, G., and Gobet, F. (2017). Does far transfer exist? Negative evidence from chess, music, and working memory training. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 26(6), 515-520. doi:10.1177/0963721417712760.
What it shows: The theoretical backbone of the skeptic position, arguing far transfer is generally rare across trained-skill domains.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "Two meta-analyses in Psychological Bulletin reached opposing headline conclusions from overlapping literatures (Bediou et al., 2018; Sala et al., 2018), a divergence driven largely by inclusion criteria, the treatment of active controls, and publication-bias correction. The most defensible reading is that habitual action gamers show a robust cross-sectional advantage (g = 0.64, 95% CI [0.53, 0.74]), that causal training effects are smaller (g = 0.30, 95% CI [0.11, 0.50]) and most reliable for top-down attention (g = 0.52), and that these effects, while modest, are broad in that they span perception, attention, and spatial cognition rather than a single trained task (Bediou et al., 2023). Notably, in the updated analysis publication bias was detected in the cross-sectional but not the intervention data set, which strengthens the case that the causal effect, though small, is not merely a publication artefact."

Reviewer objection to preempt: A reviewer will note the publication-bias problem that Bediou et al. themselves flag and the Sala et al. null. The intro should concede this openly and position the neural data as a convergent line of evidence that does not depend on resolving the behavioural meta-analytic dispute.

Verification flag: The user requested newer (2024 to 2026) updated meta-analyses, large intervention trials, or registered reports. Within budget I could not confirm a specific post-2023 large action-game intervention trial or registered report; the 2023 Bediou paper remains the current definitive meta-analysis. This gap should be filled with a targeted search before submission (see Recommendations, Stage 1).

### TASK A.2 Genre specificity

Verified citations:

1. Green, C. S., Li, R., and Bavelier, D. (2010). Perceptual learning during action video game playing. Topics in Cognitive Science, 2(2), 202-216. doi:10.1111/j.1756-8765.2009.01054.x.
What it shows: Defines action games by fast motion, high perceptual/cognitive/motor load, unpredictable events, and peripheral processing demands, and argues players form better perceptual templates (extract task statistics faster).

2. Green, C. S., and Bavelier, D. (2003). Action video game modifies visual selective attention. Nature, 423(6939), 534-537. doi:10.1038/nature01647.
What it shows: Foundational demonstration that action gaming, and short action-game training in non-players, alters visual selective attention, establishing genre-specific causal effects.

3. Bavelier, D., and Green, C. S. (2019). Enhancing attentional control: Lessons from action video games. Neuron, 104(1), 147-163. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2019.09.031.
What it shows: Articulates the three candidate game mechanics that distinguish action games: pacing (decisions under time pressure), a load on divided attention (filtering distractors and sustaining attention over a wide field), and repeated switching between divided and focused attentional modes.

4. Dale, G., Joessel, A., Bavelier, D., and Green, C. S. (2020). A new look at the cognitive neuroscience of video game play. Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences, 1464(1), 192-203. doi:10.1111/nyas.14295 (PMID 31943260).
What it shows: Argues the one-to-one mapping between genre label and mechanics has broken down as hybrid games proliferate, motivating a mechanics-based rather than label-based taxonomy.

5. Cardoso-Leite, P., Ansarinia, M., Schmuck, E., and Bavelier, D. (2021). Training cognition with video games. In K. Cohen Kadosh (Ed.), The Oxford Handbook of Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. Oxford University Press.
What it shows: Reviews behavioural and neuroimaging evidence that action games (defined as first- and third-person shooters) yield greater cognitive enhancement, especially for top-down attention, than puzzle or life-simulation games.

Supporting contrast case (verified as reference): Kuhn, S., Gleich, T., Lorenz, R. C., Lindenberger, U., and Gallinat, J. (2014). Playing Super Mario induces structural brain plasticity: gray matter changes resulting from training with a commercial video game. Molecular Psychiatry, 19(2), 265-271. doi:10.1038/mp.2013.120. This is the platformer contrast the manuscript cites (right hippocampus, right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, cerebellum) and differs from the action-game signature.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "Cognitive benefits are not a generic property of video games. They track a specific constellation of gameplay demands present in fast first- and third-person action titles, namely pacing under time pressure, a sustained load on divided attention, and repeated switching between divided and focused processing (Bavelier and Green, 2019). Because these mechanics increasingly appear in hybrid titles, the field is moving from genre labels to mechanics-based classification (Dale et al., 2020); we therefore define our action gamers by the mechanics their games impose, not by a marketing genre."

Reviewer objection to preempt: A reviewer may cite the game-factors meta-analysis (Pistono et al. / a game-factors approach, PLOS ONE 2023, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0285925) showing genre labels ("action", "strategy") are poor predictors relative to gameplay features. Turn this to advantage: it supports defining the sample by mechanics and load profile rather than by label.

### TASK A.3 Neural bases of AVG play, with emphasis on resting-state

Verified citations (resting-state and connectivity):

1. Gong, D., He, H., Liu, D., Ma, W., Dong, L., Luo, C., and Yao, D. (2015). Enhanced functional connectivity and increased gray matter volume of insula related to action video game playing. Scientific Reports, 5, 9763. doi:10.1038/srep09763.
What it shows: The single most on-point precedent. Comparing AVG experts and amateurs, the authors state verbatim: "AVG experts exhibited increased functional connectivity between the attentional and sensorimotor networks, and the experience-related enhancement was predominantly evident in the left insula, an understudied brain area." IMPORTANT: this is a task-based and diffusion study, not resting-state; a bioRxiv preprint exists (doi:10.1101/2024.09.26.615213). 4. Gong, D., Ma, W., Liu, T., Yan, Y., and Yao, D. (2019). Electronic-sports experience related to functional enhancement in central executive and default mode areas. Neural Plasticity, 2019, 1940123. doi:10.1155/2019/1940123.
What it shows: Extends the resting-state findings to esports (League of Legends experts vs amateurs), reporting enhanced functional integration in central executive and default mode areas that scales with in-game rank; useful for the esports-players angle the manuscript mentions.

5. Martinez, K., Solana, A. B., Burgaleta, M., Hernandez-Tamames, J. A., Alvarez-Linera, J., Roman, F. J., et al. (2013). Changes in resting-state functionally connected parietofrontal networks after videogame practice. Human Brain Mapping, 34(12), 3143-3157. doi:10.1002/hbm.22129.

### TASK A.4 The theoretical pivot: action games as closed-loop online control tasks

Verified citations:

1. Dosenbach, N. U. F., Fair, D. A., Miezin, F. M., Cohen, A. L., Wenger, K. K., Dosenbach, R. A. T., et al. (2007). Distinct brain networks for adaptive and stable task control in humans. PNAS, 104(26), 11073-11078. doi:10.1073/pnas.0704320104.
What it shows: Establishes, from resting-state connectivity, the dissociation of a frontoparietal network (rapid, trial-by-trial adaptive control) and a cingulo-opercular network (stable set-maintenance across a task epoch).

2. Dosenbach, N. U. F., Fair, D. A., Cohen, A. L., Schlaggar, B. L., and Petersen, S. E. (2008). A dual-networks architecture of top-down control. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 12(3), 99-105. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2008.01.001.
What it shows: The dual-network theory in review form: CON provides tonic task-set maintenance implemented in downstream sensorimotor processors; FPN adjusts control rapidly. This is the theoretical hinge linking control networks to sensorimotor output.

3. Dosenbach, N. U. F., Nardos, B., Cohen, A. L., Fair, D. A., Power, J. D., Church, J. A., et al. (2010). Prediction of individual brain maturity using fMRI. Science, 329(5997), 1358-1361. doi:10.1126/science.1194144.
What it shows: The source of the ROI/network definitions the manuscript uses (the "Dosenbach 2010" set, including the sensorimotor regions: precentral gyrus, mid-insula, SMA/pre-SMA, superior parietal). Cite this for the SMN definition.

4. Sadaghiani, S., and D'Esposito, M. (2015). Functional characterization of the cingulo-opercular network in the maintenance of tonic alertness. Cerebral Cortex, 25(9), 2763-2773. doi:10.1093/cercor/bhu072.
What it shows: Empirically ties CON activity to tonic alertness, supporting the "CON as sustained task-set/alertness maintenance broadcast to sensorimotor systems" reading.

5. Bavelier, D., Green, C. S., Pouget, A., and Schrater, P. (2012). Brain plasticity through the life span: Learning to learn and action video games. Annual Review of Neuroscience, 35, 391-416. doi:10.1146/annurev-neuro-060909-152832.
What it shows: The "learning to learn" account: action games train the extraction of task statistics and the setting of attentional templates, a domain-general capacity that could plausibly be instantiated as reconfigured control-network coordination.

Supporting nuance (flag publication status): The cingulo-opercular network is increasingly reconceptualized as an "action-mode" network integrating cognition with action (Gordon, Raichle, Dosenbach and colleagues, 2024). This is a strong fit for the "linking cognition to action" pivot, but confirm the final peer-reviewed venue and full details before citing, since only a lab preprint version was verified within budget.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "Action games are closed-loop, online control tasks: perception, decision, and motor output are continuously coupled under time pressure. The dual-network account of top-down control (Dosenbach et al., 2007, 2008) provides a natural substrate, because the cingulo-opercular network maintains task set tonically and must express that set through downstream sensorimotor processors, while the frontoparietal network adjusts control moment to moment. If sustained action-gaming trains the online broadcasting of control signals into sensorimotor systems, the expected long-term signature is precisely strengthened intrinsic coupling between CON/FPN and the sensorimotor network, consistent with a learning-to-learn reconfiguration (Bavelier et al., 2012) rather than a narrow, task-specific change."

### TASK B.1 Rest-task correspondence

Verified citations:

1. Cole, M. W., Bassett, D. S., Power, J. D., Braver, T. S., and Petersen, S. E. (2014). Intrinsic and task-evoked network architectures of the human brain. Neuron, 83(1), 238-251. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2014.05.014.
What it shows: A whole-brain architecture present across dozens of task states closely matches the resting-state architecture; task states are the intrinsic architecture plus small, consistent modifications. Licenses inferring task-relevant organization from rest.

2. Tavor, I., Parker Jones, O., Mars, R. B., Smith, S. M., Behrens, T. E., and Jbabdi, S. (2016). Task-free MRI predicts individual differences in brain activity during task performance. Science, 352(6282), 216-220. doi:10.1126/science.aad8127.
What it shows: Resting (task-free) connectivity predicts individual task-evoked activation across many domains, indicating individual task responses are largely inherent and readable from rest.

3. Gratton, C., Laumann, T. O., Nielsen, A. N., Greene, D. J., Gordon, E. M., Gilmore, A. W., et al. (2018). Functional brain networks are dominated by stable group and individual factors, not cognitive or daily variation. Neuron, 98(2), 439-452.e5. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2018.03.035.
What it shows: Across highly sampled individuals, functional networks are dominated by stable individual features, with much smaller task-state and day-to-day contributions, so resting networks are suited to measuring stable individual characteristics.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "Resting and task functional architectures are largely the same architecture (Cole et al., 2014), individual task activation is predictable from task-free scans (Tavor et al., 2016), and network organization is dominated by stable individual rather than transient state factors (Gratton et al., 2018). Measuring intrinsic connectivity therefore does not sacrifice task relevance; it isolates the stable, individual-specific backbone on which task states are built, which is exactly the level at which a durable gaming-related reconfiguration should appear."

### TASK B.2 Experience and training sculpting resting connectivity

Verified citations:

1. Lewis, C. M., Baldassarre, A., Committeri, G., Romani, G. L., and Corbetta, M. (2009). Learning sculpts the spontaneous activity of the resting human brain. PNAS, 106(41), 17558-17563. doi:10.1073/pnas.0902455106.
What it shows: Intensive perceptual learning on a shape-identification task altered resting functional connectivity between trained visual cortex and fronto-parietal attention areas, and the magnitude of the resting change tracked the amount of learning. Direct proof that training leaves a resting-state trace.

2. Guerra-Carrillo, B., Mackey, A. P., and Bunge, S. A. (2014). Resting-state fMRI: A window into human brain plasticity. The Neuroscientist, 20(5), 522-533. doi:10.1177/1073858414524442.
What it shows: Review arguing RSFC reflects the accumulated history of co-activation between regions and can index experience-dependent plasticity, surveying sensory, motor, and cognitive-training studies.

3. Harmelech, T., and Malach, R. (2013). Neurocognitive biases and the patterns of spontaneous correlations in the human cortex. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 17(12), 606-615. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2013.09.014.
What it shows: The Hebbian-like account: spontaneous resting correlations encode individual biases stored as synaptic efficacies and can be durably remodeled by intense, focused training.

4. Papadaki, E., Koustakas, T., Werner, A., Lindenberger, U., Kuhn, S., and Wenger, E. (2023). Resting-state functional connectivity in an auditory network differs between aspiring professional and amateur musicians and correlates with performance. Brain Structure and Function, 228(9), 2147-2163. doi:10.1007/s00429-023-02711-1.
What it shows: Converging cross-domain evidence that intensive skilled practice leaves a behaviorally relevant signature in task-free resting connectivity; aspiring professional musicians showed greater resting network strength and global efficiency in a task-defined auditory network than amateurs, correlated with performance.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "Intensive skilled practice is known to remodel intrinsic connectivity. Perceptual learning changes resting connectivity in proportion to the amount learned (Lewis et al., 2009), a Hebbian coactivation logic formalizes why (Harmelech and Malach, 2013; Guerra-Carrillo et al., 2014), and expertise signatures in resting connectivity have been documented in other high-practice domains such as music (Papadaki et al., 2023). Years of action gaming, which repeatedly and jointly recruit control and sensorimotor systems, should by the same logic sculpt their resting coupling."

### TASK B.3 Resting connectivity as a stable trait predicting attention and control

What it shows: A stable within-network connectivity backbone of visual and dorsal attention networks is preserved from rest to task, while attention increases top-down directed connectivity; supports the "Spadone 2015" citation in the draft and the idea that resting patterns are dynamically reconfigured to support attention.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "Intrinsic connectivity is individually stable enough to fingerprint a person (Finn et al., 2015) and structured enough to predict that person's sustained attention (Rosenberg et al., 2016) and executive function (Reineberg et al., 2015). If resting connectivity indexes stable individual differences in exactly the attentional and control faculties that action gaming is argued to enhance, then a group difference in control-to-sensorimotor coupling is a candidate trait-level substrate rather than an incidental state effect."

### TASK B.4 Honest limits and the best counterarguments

Verified citations:

1. Marek, S., Tervo-Clemmens, B., Calabro, F. J., Montez, D. F., Kay, B. P., Hatoum, A. S., et al. (2022). Reproducible brain-wide association studies require thousands of individuals. Nature, 603(7902), 654-660. doi:10.1038/s41586-022-04492-9. (Publisher correction: Nature, 605, E11, doi:10.1038/s41586-022-04692-3.)
What it shows: Using a total sample of around 50,000 individuals, brain-wide association studies (mass-univariate correlations between a single brain feature and a single behavioral/individual-difference measure across the population) need thousands of subjects for reproducibility. The abstract notes the median neuroimaging study sample size is about 25, making typical studies potentially too small; small samples inflate effects and fail to replicate.

2. Noble, S., Scheinost, D., and Constable, R. T. (2019). A decade of test-retest reliability of functional connectivity: A systematic review and meta-analysis. NeuroImage, 203, 116157. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116157.
What it shows: Mean edge-level test-retest reliability is only "poor" (mean ICC = 0.29, 95% CI [0.23, 0.36]), but reliability is substantially higher for strong within-network cortical edges and improves with more within-subject data.

3. Birn, R. M., Molloy, E. K., Patriat, R., Parker, T., Meier, T. B., Kirk, G. R., et al. (2013). The effect of scan length on the reliability of resting-state fMRI connectivity estimates. NeuroImage, 83, 550-558. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.05.099.
What it shows: Reliability improves substantially as scan length rises from 5 minutes toward 13 or more minutes; supports acquiring longer resting scans for stable estimates.

4. Gordon, E. M., Laumann, T. O., Gilmore, A. W., Newbold, D. J., Greene, D. J., Berg, J. J., et al. (2017). Precision functional mapping of individual human brains. Neuron, 95(4), 791-807.e7. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.07.011.
What it shows: With enough within-individual data, individual functional networks are highly reliable and stable, the empirical basis for the deep-phenotyping / precision-fMRI defense of small-N designs.

Prose-ready reasoning point: "The obvious objection is Marek et al. (2022): brain-wide association studies need thousands of participants. That critique targets mass-univariate correlations between a single connectivity feature and a continuous individual-difference measure across a heterogeneous population, where effect sizes are tiny and sampling variability dominates. It does not directly govern a hypothesis-driven contrast between two well-characterized groups on a pre-specified set of control-to-sensorimotor edges, evaluated with a within-sample multiverse across 280 specifications. Reliability is nonetheless a real constraint: edge-level ICCs are modest on average (mean ICC = 0.29; Noble et al., 2019), so we prioritize strong within-network edges (the most reliable), acquire sufficient scan length (Birn et al., 2013), and interpret results at the network rather than the single-edge level, consistent with precision-fMRI evidence that individual network structure is highly stable given adequate data (Gordon et al., 2017)."

Reviewer objections to preempt, and the replies:
- "N of 16 per group is underpowered (Marek 2022)." Reply: distinguish BWAS-style population correlations from group classification on pre-specified networks; emphasize the multiverse as a within-sample robustness tool, not a replacement for external replication; state the limitation explicitly and call for independent replication.
- "Resting connectivity is unreliable." Reply: reliability is edge- and length-dependent; strong within-network edges and longer scans are reliable (Noble 2019; Birn 2013; Gordon 2017).
- "Cross-sectional design cannot separate cause (gaming sculpts networks) from selection (pre-existing network profiles draw people to gaming)." Reply: acknowledge directly; cite longitudinal training precedents (Martinez 2013; Lewis 2009) as evidence the gaming-to-network direction is at least plausible, and frame the claim as a candidate substrate rather than a demonstrated cause.
